Materiality and Time
“Natural materials – stones, brick, and wood, allow our vision to penetrate their surfaces and enable us to become convinced of the veracity of matter.” Natural materials express their age and history, as well as the story of their origins and their history of human use. All matter exist in the continuum of time; the patina of wear adds the enriching experience of time to the materials of construction.
...
Buildings of modern technological age usually deliberately aim at ageless perfection, and they do not incorporate the dimension of time, or the unavoidable and mentally significant process of aging. This fear of the traces of wear and age is related to our fear of death.”

Human-Existential Question
Architecture domesticates limitless space and enables us to inhabit it, but it should likewise domesticate endless time and enable us to inhabit the continuum of time.
If we desire architecture to have an emancipating or healing role, instead of reinforcing the erosion of existential meaning, we must reflect on the multitude of secret ways in which the art of architecture is tied to the cultrural and mental reality of its time.
