################################################################
ARTIST STATEMENT
Folly · II — Rusted Tower: Aether-Scorched Earth
################################################################

============================================================
I | PRELUDE: SCORCHED EARTH—THE CIVILISATIONAL VOID LEFT BY AETHER
==================

This work takes “aether,” a hypothetical medium from the history of science, as its central metaphor. The “luminiferous aether” of classical physics has long since disappeared from modern theory, yet it still offers a language for describing invisible forces. What matters here is not whether aether ever truly existed, but humanity’s persistent need to imagine a medium through which forces—imperceptible yet continually acting upon reality—might be understood.

Unable to conceive how something immaterial could fill the entire universe, earlier generations invented “aether” to carry that thought. Today, standing beneath towering antenna masts amid dense electromagnetic activity, we are likewise unable to imagine just how noisy this apparently silent space may be.

In Folly—Rusted Tower: Aether-Scorched Earth, “scorched-earth aether” is therefore no longer merely the conjectural electromagnetic medium of the past. It becomes an invisible order spread across space. We cannot see the order itself, but the antenna towers allow us to sense the shape it leaves behind. The immense masts, guy wires and radially deployed aerials resemble aether’s skeleton surfacing through the ground, giving visible form to an otherwise invisible field of diffusion.

The danger of this wasteland consequently has two sources. One is the electromagnetic activity that pervades the site but remains beyond the reach of the naked eye. The other comes from the towers themselves: hanging steel cables, lightning strikes, induced currents and, in winter, ice that may fall from a great height. An invisible field and a visible structure jointly overshadow the land, turning it into scorched earth governed over the long term by an “aetheric order.”

Yet aether does not erode the land by its own hand.

What it truly alters is the relationship between people and space.

Danger and exclusion gradually remove people from the site, depriving it of continued maintenance and the possibility of being occupied anew. Once the factory ceased production, its neatly laid floors were left exposed beneath the sky of the antenna array. Standing water seeped into cracks over long periods; changes in temperature, erosion and repeated cycles of wetting and drying left depressions behind. Sea wind wore at exposed bricks and rubble, breaking them into ever finer particles. Over time, wind and water repeatedly transported, sorted and gathered these particles, forming linear, mottled and wave-like deposits across the ground.

These traces appear as a stratigraphic record left by the aetheric order.

Aether did not shape them directly. Rather, as its invisible order continued to act upon the site, people withdrew and maintenance came to an end, allowing wind, water, vegetation and time to take possession of the land once more.

The desolation in Folly—Rusted Tower: Aether-Scorched Earth is therefore not a motionless ruin left after a single act of destruction. It is an ongoing condition: an absence maintained by an invisible order even after people have departed.

Beneath the antenna array’s shadow, nature has not invaded civilisation. It has merely continued working after civilisation’s retreat, carrying out a long and silent dismantling on its behalf.

============================================================
II | THE WORK: THE AETHER CIRCUIT
============

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ RUIN IMAGE: SCORCHED-EARTH REACTION FIELD—DRIFT STIRRED BY GHOST SIGNALS
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Diantai Road long existed between two adjacent systems: a short-wave antenna array and a factory for glass and metal products. Their geographical proximity produced an unusual electromagnetic environment.

The short-wave communications system continuously transmitted signals into the surrounding space. At the same time, the factory’s heavy machinery, welding circuits and accumulated metal stock became part of the same electromagnetic environment. Ground, air and architecture were thus drawn into a single “aether circuit.”

With time, this agency seems to have exceeded a purely physical effect and sunk into the memory of the land and the order of the space. Dust and sand across the wasteland are scattered by the wind, gathered, then scattered again. Even when the dust settles, it merely pauses within another form of suspension.

Dust therefore becomes a measure of time:

“Today’s dust is the explosion of news;
yesterday’s dust is society adrift;
the dust of the day before is history’s sediment.”

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRUCTURE I: AETHER INTERSTICE—DEVELOPING AETHER IN A SAND TABLE
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Aether Interstice takes the relationship between the short-wave antennas and the glass-and-metal factory as its point of reference. Through an “Ætherphone” (theremin), it reconstructs an electromagnetic field of interference that can be perceived.

The complete structure is formed by the “tower” and the “interstice.”

Three sets of oscillating antennas move around a central zone. Their paths overlap, intersect and cut through space, producing a “hyper-positive space” temporarily enclosed by their trajectories. This is both the continuously changing interval between the three antenna sets and a capacitive field in perpetual flux.

The Ætherphone’s high-frequency oscillator operates continuously. As wind sets the antennas in motion, the distance and capacitive relationship between them continually change, shifting the oscillation frequency. Variations that could not otherwise be heard or seen are ultimately transduced into sound and structural vibration.

For the first time, aether acquires a form that can be watched.

The vibration travels into a sand table. At different frequencies, the grains rearrange themselves into ever-changing Chladni figures. Translated successively through frequency, sound and vibration, invisible electromagnetic action finally leaves fleeting geometries in the dust.

As the frequency changes, dispersed dust gathers once more; when it shifts again, the newly formed order breaks apart.

Here, the dust makes visible not vibration alone, but the process through which space is continually shaped by an ordering force.

Every gathering and disintegration is an act of deconstruction, reassembly and renewed deconstruction.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRUCTURE II: AETHER TOWER OF THE SCORCHED EARTH—FICTIONAL ANCHOR AND TENSILE STRUCTURE
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Aether Tower of the Scorched Earth is founded upon a deliberately sustained sense of instability.

Steel cables and detached components establish a mechanical relationship approaching tensegrity. The visible struts do not join into a continuous skeleton in the conventional sense; what actually holds the whole together is the persistent, hidden tension pulling between its parts.

Visually, the three oscillating antenna sets remain separate, as if they might lose their support at any moment.

At the foot of the tower, an inverted triangular structure with no actual apex grips a glass bead. The bead is designated a “fictional anchor”: it appears always on the verge of rolling away, yet continues to sustain the entire construction.

A nonexistent “aether” was once imagined as the medium holding the universe together. Here, an apparently unreliable glass anchor keeps the tower stable.

Both depend upon something that cannot be directly trusted to support an order visible on the surface.

These apparently dangerous limit states originated in an intensely rational engineering logic devoted to efficiency. Once the tower leaves the continuously maintained environment of civilisation and enters an unattended wasteland, however, this rational structure begins to reveal another face.

The steel cables cease to be geometric lines and disclose themselves again as bundles of entwined metal fibres. The truss ceases to be a symbol on an engineering drawing and becomes material once more: something capable of bending, rusting, fatiguing and breaking.

The tower gradually loses its status as an emblem of “permanent infrastructure.” What remains is a body still standing and already decaying.

Support and failure, reliability and danger, coexist. As the oscillating antennas flatten and deform, the tension once used to resist gravity disappears little by little.

The collapse of Aether Tower of the Scorched Earth is therefore not an instant, but a prolonged loss of tension.

Finally, when the central Aether Interstice falls and pierces the antennas and tower body, the relationships that maintained equilibrium are completely disrupted, pushing the entire structure towards the critical point at which it loses its centre of gravity.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRUCTURE III: FALLEN BABEL OF THE SCORCHED EARTH—A MILLSTONE FOR HUMAN TRACES
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

As humanity came to depend on antennas, radio and communications apparatus as media of connection, forms of exchange that were once felt directly were increasingly entrusted to technical systems built upon efficiency and reason.

The rational tools made by human hands are consequently not only extensions of civilisation; they may also become media through which desolation is produced.

Fallen Babel of the Scorched Earth emerged from this thought.

The Chinese title “Tabie Tower” mutates the name of the Tower of Babel by replacing its first syllable with the word for collapse. Its structure is inverted just as completely: the vertical order that once attempted to reach the sky is refolded after its fall into a horizontal circuit that can never leave its point of origin.

It no longer grows upwards. It begins to turn around itself.

The summit that once pointed skyward becomes the centre of a ceaseless vortex. The staircase that once drew people to a height becomes, in its fallen state, a row of teeth grazing the ground.

The tower no longer builds civilisation. It begins to grind civilisation down.

The fallen Tower of Babel thus becomes an immense, continuously operating millstone.

Again and again it turns about its origin, compressing, breaking and abrading debris, reinforcing bars, stone fragments and dust across the ground. The steps once used to build upwards now repeatedly sweep the surface, leaving superimposed and intersecting trajectories of motion.

Whenever the fragments of civilisation gather again, the millstone scatters them once more.

Danger comes not from a single sudden collapse, but from an attrition that never ceases.

Across the unattended scorched earth, Fallen Babel of the Scorched Earth repeatedly passes over the tower within it. Clusters of concrete columns at the base are gradually stripped away, shaken loose and crushed; the outer wires of the steel cables loosen under repeated friction, fatigue and finally snap.

It has no intention of destroying the tower.

It simply continues to operate according to its own absolute and repetitive order.

For precisely that reason, destruction becomes inevitable.

As its supports are exhausted one increment at a time, the whole tower finally falls to the ground like a prophecy already written into its structure.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ COMBINED STRUCTURE: THE AETHER CIRCUIT
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Aether Interstice, Aether Tower of the Scorched Earth and Fallen Babel of the Scorched Earth together form a continuously cycling “aether circuit.”

Each corresponds to a different scale within the same scorched earth:

from invisible electromagnetic action,
to visible mechanical structure,
and then to a spatial order sinking back into the land.

Aether Interstice gives form to electromagnetic action that was previously invisible. Changes in frequency set the grains gathering and dispersing, allowing an unseen field to leave temporarily visible traces in dust.

Aether Tower of the Scorched Earth projects that invisible order into spatial structure. It does not shape the land directly; through danger, isolation and the cessation of maintenance, it gradually removes people from the site, allowing nature and time to take possession of the ground once more.

Fallen Babel of the Scorched Earth continues the process. Absolute order is embodied as an artificial colossus out of control. Through endlessly repeated motion, it crushes, strips and abrades the surface, remixing the remnants of civilisation scattered across the wasteland.

Order produces chaos, and chaos reveals order anew.

Aether shapes space;
space shapes scorched earth;
scorched earth shapes dust;
and dust develops the image of aether once more.

============================================================
III | VIEWING GUIDE: GRAPHIC SCORE—AETHER SCHERZO OF THE SCORCHED EARTH
====================

In one sense, aether and windblown sand share the same qualities: neither has a fixed form, both diffuse through space, and external forces continually redistribute them.

The grain of sand therefore becomes the most immediate medium through which to watch aether.

In Aether Scherzo of the Scorched Earth, electromagnetic oscillation itself is not directly visible. What can be watched is the gathering and dispersal of grains under vibration.

The Chladni figure is consequently more than a geometric pattern left by sound. It becomes a way of recording how order temporarily gives shape to matter.

Chaos gradually forms a pattern;
the pattern dissolves into chaos once more.

The figure is never an endpoint, only the trace of a place where order briefly came to rest.

Every oscillation rearranges the grains;
every pause leaves the conditions for the change to come.

No figure can endure forever.

Radio is, in itself, an electromagnetic phenomenon present in the cosmos and in nature. Once incorporated into communication, administration and infrastructure, however, it was progressively organised into a technological order that sustains the operation of civilisation.

People have long grown accustomed to understanding radio as a stable and dependable system, rarely recognising that they live within an invisible field.

It continually pervades and penetrates, yet answers to no living thing.

It has no visible form, yet helps determine how space may be used;
it has no walls, yet can redraw the boundaries of land.

It is as though civilisation had learned a form of indifferent order from the nameless radiation crossing the universe.

Through Aether Scherzo of the Scorched Earth, Folly—Rusted Tower: Aether-Scorched Earth therefore portrays not an ancient medium that truly exists, but the invisible orders that cannot be directly perceived and nevertheless continue to shape land, civilisation and human behaviour.

“Aether” is no longer an abandoned hypothesis of physics.

It becomes a method for looking at this scorched earth.
