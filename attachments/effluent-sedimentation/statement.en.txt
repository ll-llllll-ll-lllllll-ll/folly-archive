WORK GUIDE

Folly I · Sunken Ventricle

How to Watch: The Deadwater Ventricle slowly loses its support, and the heart answers that change with ever faster blows, until it destroys itself.

Structure I | Deadwater Ventricle

The “Deadwater Ventricle” consists of a reservoir shaped as though it has burst open, the remains of a ventricle-like structure, and a pool of dead water. Together, they enact the irreversible state of the ruin at Wenzhu Dam. Destruction does not stop there: after the water chamber and the ventricle-shaped pump rupture, the water within gradually drains away. Deprived of its former support, the concrete core is compressed, fractured and driven downward by its own weight, until it finally contracts.

With dead water as the sunken ruin's final destination, the entire ventricular structure hangs suspended in a stagnant pool that no one approaches. Its surviving parts remain immersed there, continuing to corrode.

Structure II | Rust-Blood Heart

Embedded in the “reinforced-steel aorta” of the “Deadwater Ventricle,” a mechanical heart called the “Rust-Blood Heart” seals corroded metal collected from the ruin inside a glass enclosure, waiting for force and control to be supplied before it can beat. Its presence offers the “Deadwater Ventricle” one last chance to burn itself out: as fragments of the ventricle settle, cables and pulleys within its “reinforced-steel aorta” pull on the pump plate; the plate passes one final upward thrust to the “Rust-Blood Heart.” These immense forces, slow as they may appear and borrowed from the collapse itself, make the little heart assembled from fragments beat once more.

Although the Rust-Blood Heart keeps trembling in resistance to the mechanical obstructions caused by corrosion, it cannot prevent a chain of destructive reactions: excessive power, prolonged short circuits, overly rapid cycling or excessive stress may all lead to a meltdown. When the pump plate transmits more energy than the heart can bear, the impacts that once resembled the rhythm of life grow faster and more ragged, as though the corroded fragments were trying again and again to break through the walls of their enclosure. It gains life through beating, and exhausts that life in the act of beating; every impact in its attempt to break free brings it closer to its final meltdown.

When Heart's Blood Runs Dry

In the “Deadwater Ventricle,” the ruin at Wenzhu Dam is seen as the heart of a civilisation whose blood circulation is failing. Dead water trapped in the fissures of its foundations has taken the place of the “human lifeblood” that once flowed there, while the silt and filth formerly hidden beneath the surface have settled over the city's wound, like a “blood-scab of civilisation” that can never heal. As the lives and acts of maintenance that sustained the buildings gradually recede, the heart's blood and memories once poured into this place lose their foothold as well, leaving only hollow structures awaiting collapse as time passes.

In seeming contrast, the “Rust-Blood Heart” housed within the “Deadwater Ventricle” still struggles to go on beating. It is only the size of a human heart. It cannot restore circulation or reverse the ruin's fate, yet stubbornly maintains the last rhythm of life. Each beat draws its force from the ventricle's collapse, even as the heart wears itself away. Between the ever-expanding new city and the old ground slipping into oblivion, this nearly futile resistance retains a lingering remembrance of the breath of human life; through its own destruction, it leaves one last heartbeat for the human traces now disappearing.
