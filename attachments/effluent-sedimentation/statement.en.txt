################################################################
WORK GUIDE
Folly I · Sunken Ventricle
################################################################

============================================================
I | From Site to “Ventricle”
============================================================

Sunken Ventricle translates the seepage conditions of Wenzhu Dam into a body that gradually loses pressure. On site, water moves downward from the artificial lake and higher ground, passes through fissures, old foundations, and underground cavities, and gathers in the low area. The work compresses that slow loss into a mechanical relation between ventricle, blood, and heart.

Water is not decoration here; it is a structural condition. It behaves both like blood maintaining pressure and like a residue that continues leaking from a wound after urban redevelopment. Once the water is lost, the outer structure begins to lose support. Stagnant water and sediment remain as a “civilizational scab” that never fully closes the opening beneath it.

============================================================
II | Deadwater Ventricle: A Shell Losing Pressure
============================================================

The outer structure is treated as a large ventricle. Its internal water provides support and pressure; as the level falls, the concrete core slowly sinks, contracts, and cracks under its own weight.

The change does not depend on a single break. Instead, the conditions of support disappear incrementally. The shell largely keeps its shape even as its internal relationships fail. Because this collapse is difficult to perceive over a short interval, the work uses the extremely slow time of ruin as the underlying tempo of the whole system.

============================================================
III | Rust-Blood Heart: Amplifying Collapse into a Beat
============================================================

At the centre, the mechanically driven Rust-Blood Heart encloses corroded metal gathered from the ruin inside a glass vessel. It keeps beating while the fragments collide and wear against one another, so the act of “keeping alive” becomes another source of exhaustion.

Changes in the chamber below travel through cables, pulleys, a resistance slide, and variable resistance to the heart. As the structure approaches collapse, the heart’s rhythm and load shift. Settlement too slow to see is translated into a faster mechanical time that can be heard.

============================================================
IV | How to Watch: Three Times at Once
============================================================

Three scales can be followed simultaneously: hydrology and foundation change at the site are the slowest; the ventricle loses pressure at a visible but restrained pace; the mechanical heart amplifies both through continuous impacts.

The work therefore does not try to “repair” a ruined heart. It places a mechanism that still beats beside a space that has already begun to lose the conditions for circulation. The harder the system works to continue, the more wear it produces; the closer it comes to failure, the clearer its sound becomes.

Sunken Ventricle turns collapse from an end state into a process that can be watched and heard.
