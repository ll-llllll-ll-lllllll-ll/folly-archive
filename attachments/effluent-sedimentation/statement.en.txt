################################################################
ARTIST STATEMENT
Folly · I — Sunken Ruin: Ventricle
################################################################

============================================================
I | PRELUDE: DEAD WATER THROUGH THE RUIN’S FISSURES—THE BLOOD SCAB OF CIVILISATION
====================

Water is the central metaphor of Folly—Sunken Ruin: Ventricle.

At Luxelakes, water has been used to construct a way of life held at a distance from the land. Ground where no lake had existed was filled with water anew; towers and villas rose from its surface, as though only by leaving the soil beneath one’s feet could the future city acquire the respectability expected of it.

Water thus becomes a gentle and cruel boundary.

It separates more than space. It also severs the historical continuity between the new city and the land that preceded it. Former villages, farmland and local memory are pressed beneath the lake, while new residents gain, through its landscaped surface, a way of living that never requires them to confront the ground’s past directly.

Yet water has not truly erased these traces.

Long-term impoundment has altered the original relationship between surface water and groundwater. Rain, subterranean seepage and unidentified drainage move slowly through fissures in the red sandstone, old foundations and underground cavities, gradually converging on the lower terrain of Wenzhuba.

Faced with Wenzhuba’s cliff-like retaining wall, what reaches the body first is not its height, but its smell.

Damp and sickly sweet, it carries rust, sewage and an indistinct chemical odour. Mixed water seeps slowly through the cracks in the wall like a warm and putrefying breath.

Only then does it become apparent that what stands before us is not merely a retaining wall, but an urban section cut open.

Above is a city of glass curtain walls, artificial lakes and new housing. Tall, clean and seemingly complete, it continues to extend across the water.

Below lies older ground, progressively flooded, compressed and forgotten. The mixed water flows through damaged foundations, carrying silt, effluent and residual human traces downwards, where they stagnate and settle.

Water sutures the two worlds together.

The sheen of the new city and the decay of the old land can no longer remain apart. They are pressed into the same body of water and the same fissure.

Water that can no longer move gradually congeals into sludge, like a “blood scab of civilisation” covering an urban wound. It does not heal the wound; it only wraps, for a time, the filth that continues to seep from within.

The city above the water continues to expand, while the old ground beneath it is increasingly compressed, buried and forgotten.

There is no cathartic moment of destruction here.

There is only a wound that remains exposed—slowly leaking, never truly able to heal.

Folly—Sunken Ruin: Ventricle attends to this wound.

============================================================
II | THE WORK: RESONANCE OF A COLLAPSING HEART
==============

┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ RUIN IMAGE: THE DESICCATION OF “LIFEBLOOD”
└────────────────────────────────┘

In Stagnant-Water Ventricle of the Sunken Ruin, Wenzhuba is no longer treated as a ruin alone, but as the heart of a civilisation losing its capacity for circulation.

Stagnant dead water in the foundation fissures replaces the lifeblood of human life that once flowed through the site. Congealing over the wound, it becomes a “blood scab of civilisation” that refuses to fall away.

As memory, maintenance and everyday life gradually withdraw, the architecture begins to lose the internal circulation by which it supported itself.

What remains is a hollow shell that retains its outline while steadily losing internal pressure.

Against this slow collapse, the Rust-Blood Heart at the centre of the structure continues to beat.

It can neither re-establish circulation nor reverse the fate of the ruin. It can only maintain, stubbornly, the final rhythm.

Like a futile resistance that has not yet ceased, it preserves the last trace of breath for the human marks disappearing between the ever-expanding new city and the old ground retreating underground.

The viewer is thereby placed in a position resembling that of “the Fool.”

Unable to repair the heart or truly prevent its destruction, the viewer can only draw near, touch and listen, continuing to wait beside a system destined to fail.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRUCTURE I: RUST-BLOOD HEART—AN ACTIVATED RELIC
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Rust-Blood Heart takes the form of a mechanical heart, sealing corroded metal gathered from the ruin beneath a glass dome.

It resembles a relic removed from the ruin and activated once more.

At the same time, it has been “bonsaified”: miniaturised, enclosed and placed within a container for viewing, as though decay itself could be preserved as scenery.

Yet this preservation cannot halt the increase of entropy.

On the contrary, mechanical operation becomes a new source of attrition. The heart vibrates continuously, driving the corroded fragments against one another. The motion temporarily resists the blockage and coagulation produced by rust, but it also accelerates the fatigue, wear and failure of the components.

Excessive power, a prolonged short circuit, an overrapid frequency or the sustained accumulation of stress may all push the heart into overload and even meltdown.

The texture of decay and the force of new life are thus entangled.

It beats as it wears itself away. It attempts to sustain life while using its own movement to accelerate death.

The mechanical heart repeatedly strikes the boundary of its glass vessel, as though attempting to cross the prison of this “bonsai” by means of death itself.

At the level of physical operation, the Rust-Blood Heart loses control because the structure beneath it collapses. Changes in the ruin are transmitted to the heart through mechanical linkages, continually altering the system’s frequency and load until it enters a state of overload.

At the symbolic level, the same process corresponds to the dissipation of maintenance, responsibility and order within the ruin.

Once-regulated machinery enters disordered and dangerous motion until it destroys itself, also bringing its fetish character—as an object preserved and displayed—to complete failure.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRUCTURE II: STAGNANT-WATER VENTRICLE OF THE SUNKEN RUIN—A SHELL UNABLE TO SUPPORT ITSELF
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Unlike the high-frequency beating of Rust-Blood Heart, the Stagnant-Water Ventricle of the Sunken Ruin surrounding it presents another kind of time.

It does not lose control violently. It slowly loses support.

Within this structure, the wall-like outer shell is understood as an immense “ventricle.” The water inside assumes the role of “blood,” maintaining the pressure and support that once existed between the structural elements.

As the water continues to drain away, the ventricle gradually dries out.

What the water leaves behind is not an empty space, but dead water and sediment stagnating at the lowest points.

At the same time, the concrete core within the ventricle steadily loses its support. Under its own weight, it slowly contracts, sinks and cracks.

Stagnant-Water Ventricle of the Sunken Ruin therefore presents not a complete heart, but a cardiac chamber in the process of depressurising, drying and collapsing.

Rust-Blood Heart continues to beat rapidly inside it, while the enormous ventricle surrounding it loses, little by little, the conditions required to sustain circulation.

One continues struggling to beat.

The other has already begun to dry.

If Rust-Blood Heart simulates the final struggle before destruction, Stagnant-Water Ventricle of the Sunken Ruin answers it at a nearly motionless pace:

an individual life may struggle, a machine may continue to beat, and a building may be repeatedly reinforced; yet before a longer measure of time, these efforts remain only provisional means of holding a shape together.

When the view expands to geological time, the building itself is no more than a wave briefly rising from the surface of the earth.

It rises, overturns and finally returns to stillness.

┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ COMBINED STRUCTURE: RUIN-HEART VARIATIONS
└────────────────────────────────┘

The entire process of collapse is continuously recorded by a mechanical system of steel cables, pulley blocks, a resistance slider plate and a variable-resistance column.

The disordered force of collapse is thereby translated into ordered mechanical movement.

As the concrete core presses downwards and stress in the reinforcing bars gradually diminishes, the pulley system transmits the change to the resistance slider plate, causing it to move slowly along its track.

Collapse is no longer a single structural failure, but a process that can be measured, amplified and perceived.

Gravity and machinery, entropy and order, pull continually against one another.

The structure attempts to remain stable while moving continuously towards disintegration. That disintegration is then transmitted back through the mechanical system to Rust-Blood Heart, changing its rhythm and load.

The closer the structure below comes to collapse, the more urgently the central heart beats.

A destruction originally too slow to perceive is thus translated into another, audible temporality.

============================================================
III | VIEWING GUIDE: GRAPHIC SCORE—VARIATIONS FOR THE HEART OF A SUNKEN RUIN
====================

The collapse of Stagnant-Water Ventricle of the Sunken Ruin is so slow that it is almost imperceptible on the scale of human sensation.

The Rust-Blood Heart at the centre of the structure therefore assumes another narration of time.

It continues to strike the inner wall of the glass with an increasingly urgent rhythm, amplifying the changes taking place below.

Like the second hand of a clock, it gives an audible scale to subsidence, fracture and changes in stress that the naked eye cannot distinguish.

The ruin, the Folly and the ruin bonsai thereby correspond to three temporal systems at different scales:

the ruin at the scale of the city settles slowly;
the garden-scale structure continues to collapse;
the mechanical heart beats at high speed.

Together they form a set of variations driven by collapse.

From macrocosm to microcosm, from landscape to machine, destruction is transmitted layer by layer.

First, the withdrawal of local memory and daily life gradually turns Wenzhuba into residual space at the city’s edge.

Then the lifeblood of human habitation stagnates into dead water, forming between foundations and fissures the “blood scab of civilisation” that covers the wound.

Finally, even the mechanical heart that symbolises life and continues to beat cannot escape the outcome of disorder, overload and meltdown.

Folly—Sunken Ruin: Ventricle therefore does not ask how a ruin might be repaired.

It confronts another question:

once a human-made thing has lost its use value, the will to maintain it and its investment of desire, are we still willing to remain beside it and listen to its final heartbeat all the way through?

At that moment, the viewer finally becomes the “Fool” who pauses beside it.

It is as though the ruin’s heart were held in the Fool’s invisible hand.

The Fool presses it—

not to repair it,
nor to destroy it,

but as a probe without a definite purpose:

“If pressing brings it back to life, perhaps there will be a miracle;
if pressing breaks it, then so be it.”
